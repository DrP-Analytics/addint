About

Who runs ADDINT

ADDINT is built and curated by Shanli Parnia, MD — a physician board-certified in Addiction Medicine by the American Board of Preventive Medicine (ABPM, 2025), with additional board certifications in Internal Medicine (ABIM, 2022) and Obesity Medicine (ABOM, 2023).

She practices at Loretto Hospital, a safety-net community hospital on Chicago's West Side, where she oversees the inpatient detoxification service and cares for patients across inpatient and outpatient internal medicine. She mentors psychiatry resident physicians and medical students, and participates in the hospital's Institutional Review Board.

Research on the emerging drug supply

Her published work on emerging substances includes:

  • Xylazine in the Opioid Epidemic: A Systematic Review of Case Reports and Clinical Implications — Cureus, 2023 (PMID: 37009344).
  • Neptune's Fix — Gas-station Heroin and Its Impact: A Systematic Review and Post-Hoc Analysis of Tianeptine Exposure Cases — BMC Public Health, in press; poster, APA Mental Health Services Conference, Baltimore, Sept 2024.
  • Treatment Modalities for Internet Addiction in Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review of RCTs — J Clin Med, 2023 (PMID: 37176784).

Training

Dr. Parnia completed internal medicine residency at Morehouse School of Medicine, rotating at Grady Memorial Hospital and the Atlanta VA, where she led COVID-19 clinical outcomes research as principal investigator. She interned at Nassau University Medical Center on Long Island during the first wave of the pandemic. She earned her M.D. from the American University of the Caribbean and holds a combined B.S. in Microbiology and Oceanography from the University of British Columbia.

Why ADDINT exists

The clinicians treating the drug supply's casualties are the last to hear what is in it. Xylazine was in the fentanyl supply for years before most inpatient teams learned to recognize its wound care and withdrawal profile. Medetomidine displaced it before the last CME cycle finished. Nitazenes, then orphines, then whatever comes next — the surveillance data exist, in public feeds, in different formats, in different agencies. ADDINT is a translation layer: public sources in, one page of clinical bedside language out. Free. No accounts. No tracking. Every card links its primary source.
